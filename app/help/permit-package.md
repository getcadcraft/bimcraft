## What you'll do

Build one zip for a statutory submission (when the authority's list comes from a regulation pack it also holds the approval steps and the submission format): a cover, the comments a reviewer is likely to make (written from the checks, not guessed), the regulation report when the Building regulations add-on is on, the model, and the list of supporting documents your approving authority asks for.

This is a guide made from the model. The registered professional and the authority decide what is required and what is accepted.

## Steps

1. With a model open, open {{ui:Permit package}} in the left panel.
2. Type the project's name, address and applicant, and list your sheets and forms, one per line.
3. Under {{ui:Supporting documents}}, choose the {{ui:Authority's list}}. The lists come from regulation packs: add the Building regulations add-on and tick the pack for your place in Settings › Building regulations (a pack can hold the rules, an approval process, a submission format, drawing conventions and standard notes). The general list works without it.
4. Read the list. Each document is {{ui:Linked}} (with the sheet and why), {{ui:Supplied by you}}, {{ui:Does not apply}} or {{ui:Missing}}.
5. For a paper you supply yourself (a form, a title deed, a fee receipt) choose {{ui:Supplied by me}}. For an item marked "if it applies" that does not, choose {{ui:Does not apply here}}. To link a document to a sheet yourself, choose the sheet.
6. Open {{ui:Approval process}} and {{ui:Submission format}} under the list to read the steps and what to hand in (sheet sizes, file formats, copies). A fee or time frame shows only when the pack's source states it, with the page and the date it was read.
7. Click {{ui:Build the permit package (zip)}}. The steps and the format are on the cover too.

## How a document is linked

A document is linked to a sheet that holds the right kind of view (a floor plan, an elevation, a section), to a sheet that holds the right schedule (the door schedule, for a door schedule), or to a sheet whose title says so (a sheet titled "Site plan"). When the model has the rows but no sheet holds the schedule, or the site is drawn but no sheet is titled as a site plan, the item stays missing and says why.

## Good to know

- **Every authority's list is unverified by a local practitioner.** Each was read from the published source it cites, on the date it shows, and written in our own words. Requirements change: check the authority's own current list.
- What the set still lacks, or a sheet that is not the size, orientation or scale the pack asks for, shows in the Problems list as a note. Notes are advice: they never stop the package.
- Missing documents never stop the package. They are listed on the cover and in `04-supporting-documents.csv`.
- The lists are data, not code, inside regulation packs: South Africa (National Building Regulations, regulation A2), Cape Town (with the South Africa region added from the Library), England (national information requirements), Portland in Oregon, a MADE-UP teaching pack, and a general list that is no authority's. Anyone can write a pack for their own place.
- Your choices are kept in the model, so they are there when you open it again, and each is one step you can undo.
