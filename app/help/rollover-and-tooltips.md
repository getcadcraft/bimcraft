## What you'll do

Learn a tool's shortcut by pointing at it, and read what something on the plan is without picking it.

## Steps

1. Point at a tool, for example {{ui:Wall}} in the Draw bar. Almost at once a tooltip shows its name and its shortcut (**Wall**, **W**).
2. Keep the pointer there. A moment later one line under it says what the tool does.
3. Open a plan, with the {{ui:Select}} tool on. Rest the pointer on a wall. A tooltip by the pointer shows its type, its level, its length and thickness, and whether it is straight or skew.
4. Move to a door, a window, a room or a dimension to read that one instead. Nothing is selected.

## What the tooltip of a tool shows

- **Stage 1**: the name of the command in the menu style you use, and its keys in that style. With a command line on screen it also shows the word to type when the command has no key.
- **Stage 2**: one line of description. It is the text of the command's own button, so every place the command appears (the Draw bar, a ribbon, a menu style, the tool rows of a layout) says the same.
- A tool that is off shows why instead.

## What rollover shows

- **Wall**: type, level, length and thickness, and **Straight**, **Skew** (with the angle) or **Curved**. Straight means square to the main direction of the walls it is joined to: a building turned on the page is still straight. A wall on its own is straight.
- **Door or window**: type, level, width and height, the sill height of a window, and the wall it is in.
- **Room**: name and number, level, and the area as the plan labels it.
- **Dimension**: its length, whether it is the whole wall or the part between openings, which face it is measured from, and the wall it belongs to.

## Good to know

- Rollover works with the Select tool. While a Draw tool runs, the tool shows its own marks instead.
- It goes when you move off the thing, press a mouse button, turn the wheel or press a key.
- Tooltips never appear on touch.
