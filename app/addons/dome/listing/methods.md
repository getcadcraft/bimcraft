The dome is a surface of revolution about a vertical axis. Its curve is a circular arc (base radius a, rise H, sphere radius (a² + H²) / 2H) or half an ellipse (semi-axes a and H). The diameter and height describe the inflated form; the foam and the concrete lie inside it at fixed distances along the normal, the concrete growing evenly from the crown thickness to the base thickness. Every part is a closed profile turned about the axis, so a volume is the profile's area times the path of its centroid (Pappus) and a surface is a sum of cone frustums. The solids are drawn with 48 straight pieces along the curve; the quantities are taken with 512, within a few parts in a million of the closed forms for a sphere and an ellipsoid. An opening is cut between two radial planes from its sill to its head.

The forces start from membrane theory for a shell of revolution under loads that are the same all the way round, on the concrete's mid-surface: the force along the curve is the load above a parallel divided by 2π r0 sin φ, and the force round the dome follows from N_φ / r1 + N_θ / r2 = −Z, with r1 and r2 the principal radii of curvature. Own weight is density times thickness; snow is your figure on plan. Each load is multiplied by its load factor first (1 with no spec). A steel area is a force divided by the stress you typed. The pressure under the ring beam is the weight of dome, stem wall and beam over the beam's area.

Wind and earthquake vary round the dome as the cosine of the angle. For such a load the membrane forces of any shell of revolution follow from statics: the part above a parallel is pushed sideways by F and turned over by M, and the force along the curve is −M / (π r0² sin φ), the shear that plus F / (π r0). For a sphere under the textbook wind pattern this is Timoshenko's closed form. Bending comes from the conical-frustum element of a shell of revolution (three movements a node, about 280 pieces over the curve), linear elastic and uncracked, checked against a clamped cylinder and a pressurised sphere. Buckling is the classical pressure 2Et² / (√(3(1 − ν²)) r1 r2) against the load pressing on the shell. An opening's forces are the membrane forces over its width and height.

No norm value is in the generator. A spec side add-on carries them as data, and each value records its standard, clause, the page it was read on and whether that was the standard's own text. The generator reads only values it can use as a number in the right unit; sentences are shown, not applied.
