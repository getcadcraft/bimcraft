A guide, not a compliance document. It does not fill in or register a certificate of compliance. The energy consultant and the building department decide what complies.

It checks the building envelope only. Heating, cooling, ducts, water heating, ventilation, solar panels, battery readiness, quality insulation installation, the whole-house fan and roofing reflectance are listed as not checked.

It does not do the performance approach. That is a simulation of the whole house against energy budgets (source energy and long-term system cost), and the code requires software the Energy Commission has approved. The 2025 code no longer uses the Energy Design Rating score of earlier editions.

New single-family houses only: not additions, alterations, multifamily or nonresidential buildings. Wood framed walls only; mass walls, steel framing and below-grade walls are not checked. Every door in an exterior wall is treated as a swinging door that is not glazed. Skylights are not found. Exceptions in the code are not applied. A level that is turned against the model's axes gives wrong window directions.

The CBECC project file is a starting file, not a compliance result. It holds the envelope only: no heating, cooling, ducts, water heating, ventilation, solar panels or battery, no garage, no window shading, no number of bedrooms. It assumes a roof pitch, roofing, floor framing and crawl space height, and says so. Only a vented attic over a raised floor or a slab can be written so far. We have checked the file against the program's published data model, and the same check passes the Commission's 24 example houses, but we have not opened the file in the program itself.

The wall U-factor given to the model is the code table's whole-wall figure for each exterior wall type you describe; it overwrites a U-factor already on those walls. The ceiling's U-factor (from the attic roof table) goes to every roof element and a raised floor's (from the raised-floor tables) to the lowest level's floor slabs, when you press their buttons; a flat ceiling slab under an attic and a cathedral ceiling are not given one. A slab on the ground gets its F-factor per foot of edge shown, never written as a U-factor. The sun through the windows is worked out with the loads under a clear sky on the Daylight tab's date (21 July unless changed) when the model has a site or a typed latitude and longitude.
