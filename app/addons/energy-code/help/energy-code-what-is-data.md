## What a regulation pack holds

A pack holds **numbers, units, clause numbers and short labels written by us**, and says where each number was read. Nothing else. The Regulations add-on checks your model against those numbers and shows the clause beside every result, so you can look the rule up in the code itself.

## What it never holds

- **The code's text.** California's building standards are thirteen parts (Title 24). They are published by the International Code Council, the National Fire Protection Association and IAPMO under their own copyright claims, and are free to read only on their own viewers. We list the parts and link to those viewers; we do not copy, summarise or host the text, and the add-on is never a copy of the code.
- **A result that counts as compliance.** The 2025 Energy Code has no Energy Design Rating. Its performance path works in source energy and long-term system cost and must be run in software the Energy Commission has approved. The Energy code check does the prescriptive package and the mandatory minimums only, and says so.
- **Local amendments.** A city or county can amend the code. The California pack is the state's.

## What the California packs hold

- **Energy:** Table 150.1-A, the mandatory minimums and the assembly U-factor tables of the 2025 Energy Code, read by script from the Commission's own documents (see *Energy code check: getting started*).
- **The Regulations add-on's California pack:** the guard height, **42 in.** (CRC R321.1.2; the model code says 36 in.), which a drawn guard is now checked against; the electrical rules under the **California Electrical Code**'s clauses (210.52 and 210.70), not the model code's chapter 39, which California does not use; and cited numbers from the residential code's California-only sections, CALGreen and the wildland-urban interface code. The numbers the checker does not test yet are listed with their clause, so they are there to read.
- **A reference list** of the thirteen parts with their publishers, dates and links.

## Read the source line

Each rule's note says whether its number was **read on an official page** or **read on a secondary copy**. The official viewers block automated reading, so most residential-code numbers were read on a full-text copy and the amendment status on the official table. A number read on a secondary copy is a guide: confirm it on the publisher's page, whose link is in the note. The packs have not been reviewed by a California professional. The registered professional and the local authority decide.
