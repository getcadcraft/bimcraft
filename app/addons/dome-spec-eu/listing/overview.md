This is the Europe spec for the Dome generator: a data pack of what the Eurocodes set for a thin concrete shell. Cover, the strength class, the minimum steel ratio and bar spacing come from EN 1992-1-1, read in its 2004 edition, as the recommended values that a national annex may change. What EN 1992-2 says on analysing shells, EN 14487 on sprayed concrete, EN 1991 on snow and wind, EN 13501-1 on fire classes and EN 1997-1 on frost is there to read. Switch it on and pick it in the Dome generator's Spec section: its values fill the boxes you left empty, and the design is checked against the ones it holds as numbers.

Every value names its standard and clause, and says whether we read it in the standard's own text, in a document that quotes it, or could not read it at all (then there is no value). EN 1992-1-1:2004 barely mentions shells: it gives no minimum thickness and no dome buckling rule, so the steel and spacing values are its slab rules. No sprayed concrete strength class was read. The snow standard read has no clause for domes. It is not a substitute for the standards or your national annex, and a licensed engineer must design and check the dome.
