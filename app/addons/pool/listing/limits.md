It is a design aid, not an engineering approval, and it does not design the pool: nothing is checked for strength, ground water, uplift, cracking, entrapment or electrical safety, and we accept no liability for what is built from it. A licensed engineer must design and check the pool. The steel is an estimate from a ratio, or a simple bar schedule (one layer each way, lap and stock length as starting figures): never a reinforcement design. The pump head is your figure unless you ask for it to be calculated from the routed pipes; that adds friction (Hazen-Williams), bends and your filter-and-valves figure, with roughness, bend factor and equipment loss as starting figures that are not from a standard, and it leaves out pump height, a dirty filter and valve throttling. The balance tank is a share of the pool volume that you set, not a calculation. Any outline, but one straight floor slope along the length; on a side other than the shallow end the bottom of a flight follows the floor along its middle. The pipes are one connected network with ports, but the machine room pipes are laid out square by fixed offsets, not checked for clashes, with no valves, and the head loss counts the routed pipes only (not the short tails or the room pipes). A gate's hardware is not drawn. Indoor pools get no ventilation or dehumidifying. Inputs are in millimetres or feet and inches. Translated into 75 languages; the new words of 0.11.1 are English until the next language round. It has been tested against hand calculations and read back with our own IFC reader, not built, not checked by an engineer, and the IFC file has not been opened in another vendor's program.
